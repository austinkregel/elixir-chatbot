153 defmodule Hypothesis do
154 @moduledoc """
155 A testable claim derived from a research question.
156
157 Following the scientific method, a hypothesis must be:
158 - **Falsifiable**: Can be proven false with evidence
159 - **Testable**: Evidence can be gathered to evaluate it
160 - **Specific**: Clear enough to test
161
162 A hypothesis cannot be "proven true" - it can only be supported
163 by evidence or falsified by contradicting evidence.
164
165 ## Predictions
166
167 Each hypothesis includes a prediction - an "If/Then" statement that
168 describes the expected results if the hypothesis is true:
169
170 > "If the hypothesis is true, then the results of the experiment will be..."
171
172 If predictions are confirmed, the hypothesis is supported.
173 If predictions are not supported, the hypothesis is falsified.
174
175 ## Replication
176
177 Repeating the experiment (finding multiple sources) increases confidence.
178 We should not expect exactly the same answer each time - variation is normal.
179 Replication enables us to see variation and obtain an average result.
180
181 ## States
182
183 - `:untested` - No evidence gathered yet
184 - `:testing` - Currently gathering evidence
185 - `:supported` - Evidence supports the hypothesis (not proven!)
186 - `:falsified` - Contradicting evidence disproves the hypothesis
187 - `:inconclusive` - Mixed or insufficient evidence
188
189 ## Example
190
191 hypothesis = Hypothesis.new(
192 "Paris is the capital of France",
193 derived_from: "What is the capital of France?",
194 prediction: "If Paris is the capital, then authoritative sources will confirm this."
195 )
196 """

357 supporting_count = length(hypothesis.supporting_evidence)
358 contradicting_count = length(hypothesis.contradicting_evidence)
359 total = supporting_count + contradicting_count
360
361 if total == 0 do
362 %{hypothesis | confidence: 0.0, confidence_level: :none}

364 # Base confidence from pass rate (what percentage of evidence supports)

373
374 # Penalty for low sample size (need at least 5 samples for reliable results)
375 sample_size_factor = min(total / 5.0, 1.0)
376
377 # Combined confidence:
378 # - Pass rate is the primary factor (60% weight)
379 # - Reliability adjusts quality (20% weight)
380 # - Sample size matters (10% weight)
381 # - Source diversity bonus (10% weight)
382 confidence =
383 (pass_rate * 0.6 +
384 reliability_factor * 0.2 +
385 sample_size_factor * 0.1 +
386 source_diversity_bonus)

429 defmodule Investigation do
430 @moduledoc """
431 Represents a scientific investigation testing one or more hypotheses.
432
433 An investigation follows the scientific method:
434 1. Formulate hypotheses from questions
435 2. Gather evidence from independent sources
436 3. Evaluate hypotheses against evidence
437 4. Report conclusions (supported, falsified, or inconclusive)
438
439 ## Experimental Variables
440
441 From the scientific method, experiments involve three types of variables:
442
443 - **Independent Variable**: What we vary (the sources we query)
444 - **Dependent Variable**: What we measure (the findings/claims extracted)
445 - **Constants**: What we hold fixed (NLP pipeline, corroboration rules)
446
447 ## Control Treatment
448
449 A control treatment provides a baseline for comparison. In our context,
450 this could be:
451 - Existing facts in the database (do new findings agree?)
452 - Known reliable sources (Wikipedia, encyclopedias)
453
454 ## Replication
455
456 We require multiple independent sources (replication) to increase
457 confidence. Variation between sources is normal - replication helps
458 us see this variation and obtain a consensus.
459
460 ## Key Principles
461
462 - **Falsifiability**: Hypotheses can be disproven by contradicting evidence
463 - **Cannot Prove True**: Only support with evidence, never absolute proof
464 - **Accumulation**: Knowledge builds through many investigations
465 """

866 defp extract_entity(question, default_topic) do
